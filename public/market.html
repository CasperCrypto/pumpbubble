How to read the map

Size is market capitalisation

A bubble's area is proportional to the coin's market cap on a compressed scale. Bitcoin genuinely is orders of magnitude larger than most coins, so a raw scale would render everything else as invisible dots — the scale is log-compressed so the whole market stays readable at once.

Colour is price change

Green means the price rose over the selected timeframe, red means it fell, and the intensity tracks the size of the move. Switch between 5 minutes, 1 hour, 6 hours and 24 hours: a 24-hour figure means something very different for an established coin than for one launched this morning.

Gold means somebody paid

A gold bubble is a paid boost. Its size comes from what was paid, not from market cap, and it is labelled so it can never be mistaken for a genuinely large coin. Paid bubbles are capped at a quarter of the map, and every boost ever sold is published with its transaction in the public ledger. Boosts apply to Solana tokens only, so nothing on this page can be bought.

Questions

Where does the data come from?

Market caps, prices, changes and coin art come from CoinGecko for the wider market, and from pump.fun directly for the Solana launchpad map. Nothing is estimated or filled in.

How often does it update?

The market view refreshes continuously; the underlying data is re-fetched every couple of minutes, which is far faster than market caps of this size actually move. The pump.fun map refreshes every few seconds, because those tokens move that fast.

Is it free?

Yes, with no account. You can also embed the map on your own site for free.

How is this different from a normal market table?

A table makes you read a hundred rows to notice that the market is mostly red today. A bubble map tells you in one glance, and shows you relative size at the same time. The table above is here for when you want the exact numbers.
